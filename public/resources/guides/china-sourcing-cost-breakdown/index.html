China sourcing buyer guide

The cheapest China quotation is not always the lowest landed procurement cost.

A sourcing decision should compare the cost of obtaining usable goods at the required place and time, not only the supplier's unit price. Different suppliers push cost into tooling, packaging, defects, minimum quantities, transport, payment timing and buyer-side labor. Making those components visible creates a better commercial comparison.

Buyer control points

A process that can be checked, not guessed.

Use this guide as an operating framework and adapt the depth of control to product risk, order value, customization and destination-market requirements.

Working checklist

  • Unit price
  • Tooling/setup
  • Samples
  • Packaging
  • Testing/inspection
  • Sourcing/management fee if applicable
  • China-side warehouse handling
  • Freight
  • Import duties/taxes as applicable
  • Destination charges
  • MOQ inventory exposure
  • Expected defect/rework exposure
  • Buyer-side management effort
01

Separate recurring and non-recurring supplier costs

Unit price is recurring; molds, fixtures, setup, design work, certification work, artwork plates or special samples may be one-time or periodic. Keep them separate so a short pilot order and a long production program can be evaluated differently.

02

Include packaging and pack-out

Packaging affects both purchase price and logistics. Retail boxes, inserts, protective foam, pallets, reinforced cartons and kitting add direct cost but may reduce damage or buyer-side labor. Compare the same packaging baseline across suppliers.

03

Account for quality-control and failure cost

Inspection, testing and supplier verification are visible costs. Defects, rework, returns, emergency replacements and customer complaints are less visible but potentially much larger. Higher-risk products justify more prevention and evidence before shipment.

04

Model MOQ and inventory exposure

A lower unit price at a much higher MOQ can increase cash tied in stock, storage cost and obsolescence risk. Compare total purchase commitment and realistic sell-through or project demand. For new products, the option to run a smaller controlled trial may be worth a higher initial unit cost.

05

Add China-side handling and consolidation

If goods move through a warehouse for receiving, inspection, relabeling, kitting or consolidation, include those activities. Consolidation may reduce international shipment fragmentation, but it should be evaluated against storage, handling and waiting time.

06

Model freight, import cost and destination handling separately

Freight rates and import charges vary by route, product and destination. Keep them as separate estimates rather than hiding them inside supplier price. Use the correct customs classification and destination rules through qualified logistics or customs resources when those costs materially affect the decision.

07

Include the cost of management complexity

Ten very cheap suppliers can create more communication, payment, quality and receiving work than three well-controlled suppliers. Procurement labor is real cost. Supplier rationalization and standardized specifications can improve total economics even when a selected vendor is not the absolute lowest quote on every line.
