Pre-Shipment Inspection in China: A Buyer’s Checklist Before Shipment

A pre-shipment inspection (PSI) checks a defined portion of a finished order against buyer-approved requirements before release. The inspection brief—not photos alone—sets what is checked, how samples are selected, how findings are recorded and who makes the shipment decision. A PSI is evidence from a defined inspection, not a guarantee that every unit is defect-free.

01

Define the acceptance baseline before inspection

Provide the approved drawing, sample, specification, order quantity, packaging requirements and important test methods. If different documents conflict, resolve the conflict before inspection. An inspector cannot make a reliable release decision against an undefined standard.

02

Separate critical, functional and cosmetic checks

Identify characteristics that make the product unsafe or unusable, those that affect normal function, and those that affect appearance or retail presentation. This helps the inspection focus effort where defects matter most rather than spending equal time on every characteristic.

03

Use appropriate sampling and measurement

The inspection quantity and sampling method should reflect product type, lot size and buyer risk tolerance. For dimensional parts, define which dimensions need measurement and the instrument or resolution expected. For functional products, specify the actual operation to be tested rather than a vague 'function OK' checkbox.

04

Verify quantity, SKU and configuration

Wrong configuration can be as damaging as a physical defect. Confirm model, color, size, electrical version, plug, cable length, accessories, barcode and quantity by SKU. Mixed orders should be reconciled against the packing list before shipment release.

05

Inspect packaging as part of product quality

Check retail boxes, protective materials, carton strength, labels, marks and packing quantity. For fragile or heavy products, verify whether the packaging reflects the agreed transport risk. Damaged export packaging can create loss even when the product left the factory correctly.

06

Make the release decision explicit

The report should lead to a buyer decision: accept, accept with concession, rework, sort, reinspect or reject. Record defects, quantities and corrective actions. Do not let a failed inspection become a negotiation conducted only in messaging apps with no final disposition.

07

Compare pre-shipment inspection with the controls around it

These controls answer different buyer questions. Supplier qualification, production checks, finished-goods sampling and loading checks are separate scopes; one does not automatically replace another. Confirm the actual provider, timing and written scope before treating any adjacent control as included in a service.

08

Schedule PSI when the lot is inspectable and before release

Agree the inspection window with the factory and buyer before the ship date. The defined lot should be available for the agreed checks, the approved product and packaging references should be on hand, and the buyer should leave practical time for a decision, corrective work and any reinspection. Do not treat a fixed production-completion percentage as a universal rule: readiness depends on the order, sampling plan, product and contract. Confirm access, the exact lot, and who can authorize shipment release.

09

AQL sampling needs an agreed plan—not a number copied from a chart

ISO 2859-1:2026 describes sampling schemes indexed by acceptance quality limit (AQL) for lot-by-lot inspection by attributes. An AQL label by itself does not tell a buyer which sample was selected or what decision rule applies. The purchase order or inspection brief should identify the agreed standard and edition, lot and characteristics, inspection level or plan where applicable, random sample-selection method, defect classes, and acceptance/rejection criteria. Use the applicable official standard and a competent quality professional for plan values; do not paste a generic sample-size table into a contract. A sample-based acceptance result is not a promise of zero defects in the lot.

10

Make the inspection brief specific enough to reproduce

Identify the product and revision, lot size, quantity by SKU, approved drawing or sample, critical dimensions and tolerances, functional test steps, appearance references, labels and barcodes, accessories, retail packaging and export-carton requirements. State which items need measurement or a functional test, how a failure is recorded, what photos or instrument readings the buyer needs, and who receives the report. Resolve conflicting specifications before the inspection date.

11

Read the report as evidence with a defined scope

A useful report states the date and site, order or lot identifier, quantity and sample-selection basis, the requirements checked, the test or measurement method, findings by defect class, observed quantities, supporting photos or readings, limitations and final status against the agreed plan. Ask the inspector to distinguish observed facts from conclusions. A PSI report does not replace laboratory testing, destination-market certification, a full-unit inspection, supplier financial due diligence or transport-damage prevention unless those items were separately defined and completed.

12

Decide what happens after a failed or limited inspection

Before production, agree who can pause shipment and who has final release authority. If findings miss the agreed criteria, record the affected SKU and quantity, defect evidence, proposed correction, responsible party and deadline. The buyer may need to choose—under the contract—between rework, sorting, reinspection, a documented concession, partial release or rejection. An inspection finding alone does not decide refunds, liability or supplier acceptance; those depend on the parties’ agreement and applicable terms.

Frequently asked questions

Questions buyers should settle before release.

What is a pre-shipment inspection in China?

It is an agreed inspection of a defined finished-goods lot before release or shipment. The brief sets the sample method, product requirements, evidence and decision rules; the inspector reports findings for the buyer's decision.

When should I book a pre-shipment inspection?

Agree the window with the factory and buyer early enough for the lot to be ready and accessible, and leave time before shipment for review, correction and any reinspection. The correct timing depends on the order and agreed scope.

Does AQL mean that a certain number of defects is allowed in my order?

Do not interpret an AQL value in isolation as a defect allowance or a zero-defect promise. The agreed sampling plan, lot, sample and acceptance/rejection rules determine the sample-based decision. Put the exact plan and criteria in the order brief.

Does passing PSI prove that every item is defect-free?

No. A sample-based result describes the inspected sample under the agreed plan. It does not prove every unit is defect-free or replace other testing, compliance checks or transit controls.

Who decides whether the shipment is released?

The buyer or the person named in the purchase agreement should retain the release decision. Confirm in writing who may accept a concession, request correction, order reinspection or authorize a partial release.

Official references

Verify the current standard and requirements.
