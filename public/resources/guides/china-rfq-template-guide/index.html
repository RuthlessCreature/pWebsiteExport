China sourcing buyer guide

A good RFQ makes suppliers quote the same job.

The purpose of an RFQ is not simply to obtain a price. It should reduce ambiguity enough that two supplier quotations can be compared on the same technical and commercial baseline. The better the RFQ, the less time the buyer spends discovering hidden assumptions after sample approval or purchase order release.

Buyer control points

A process that can be checked, not guessed.

Use this guide as an operating framework and adapt the depth of control to product risk, order value, customization and destination-market requirements.

Working checklist

  • Drawing/spec revision
  • Quantity by SKU
  • Target market
  • Unit price
  • Tooling/setup cost
  • Sample cost and lead time
  • Production lead time
  • MOQ
  • Packaging specification
  • Incoterm and named place
  • Payment terms
  • Quality/test requirements
  • Required documentation
  • Quotation validity
01

Define exactly what is being quoted

Use a drawing, BOM, photo, reference link or written specification and give it a revision or date. If some details are intentionally open, label them as supplier-proposal items. Avoid sending different suppliers different versions of the requirement during the same comparison round.

02

State quantity and expected order pattern

Ask for the quotation at realistic quantities. If you are evaluating a trial order and a later production quantity, request both. For multi-SKU programs, list the quantity by SKU rather than giving only a total. This helps expose MOQ constraints and price breaks without forcing suppliers to guess your commercial plan.

03

Separate unit price from one-time costs

Tooling, molds, fixtures, artwork setup, samples, testing and special packaging can distort unit-price comparisons. Ask suppliers to separate recurring and non-recurring costs. For custom tooling, clarify ownership, maintenance, storage and what happens if the project moves or ends.

04

Make packaging part of the RFQ

Specify retail packaging, inner packs, export cartons, pallet requirements, labels, barcodes and shipping marks where known. If packaging is undecided, request a standard proposal and carton data. Packaging changes can materially affect price, dimensional weight, damage rate and container utilization.

05

Ask for lead-time milestones, not one number

Separate sample lead time, tooling lead time, production lead time and time from completion to pickup readiness. For a new custom item, ask what must be approved before each clock starts. This makes a supplier's timeline easier to manage than a single optimistic delivery estimate.

06

Define the quality evidence expected

State important functional tests, dimensions, appearance standards and documentary requirements. You do not need to design a full quality plan in the first RFQ, but suppliers should know which characteristics are critical and whether inspection reports, material documents or test evidence will be part of acceptance.

07

Normalize Incoterm and quotation validity

Ask all suppliers to quote the same delivery basis where possible and state the named place or port. A shorthand such as FOB without the named location is incomplete for comparison. Record quotation validity, currency and major assumptions so later negotiation does not quietly change the baseline.
