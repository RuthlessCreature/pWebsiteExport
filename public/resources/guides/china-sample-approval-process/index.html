China sourcing buyer guide

Do not approve a sample unless you can explain exactly what was approved.

Samples are where product intent becomes physical evidence. The danger is that teams often approve a sample visually while materials, internal components, dimensions, firmware, packaging or process assumptions remain undocumented. A disciplined sample process converts the approved unit into a production baseline that can be repeated and inspected.

Buyer control points

A process that can be checked, not guessed.

Use this guide as an operating framework and adapt the depth of control to product risk, order value, customization and destination-market requirements.

Working checklist

  • Sample purpose
  • Revision identifier
  • Material/version record
  • Dimensions and fit
  • Function test
  • Appearance and finish
  • Artwork/label
  • Accessories
  • Packaging
  • Open issues
  • Approved production baseline
  • Named approval authority
01

Start with a sample brief

List the purpose of the sample: appearance, fit, functional proof, material confirmation, packaging review or full pre-production approval. A rough prototype should not accidentally become the standard for dimensions or finish that were never evaluated.

02

Give every meaningful revision an identifier

When the supplier changes material, tooling, dimensions, internal components, color, artwork or packaging, record a new revision. Use annotated photos, drawings or change notes so buyer and factory are discussing the same version. Chat timestamps are a poor substitute for controlled revisions.

03

Separate DFM proposals from silent substitutions

Suppliers often have valid manufacturing suggestions. Ask them to identify proposed changes before implementation and explain the reason, cost, timing and performance impact. This allows useful DFM while preventing a cheaper or more convenient substitution from entering the product without buyer awareness.

04

Review functional and visual requirements separately

A product may look correct and still fail function; another may function correctly while missing the required finish or retail presentation. Check dimensions, materials, interfaces, operation, appearance, logo, labeling, accessories and packaging against the intended scope.

05

Create a golden sample or equivalent reference

For products where a physical master is useful, preserve an approved reference at the factory, buyer or inspection location and identify it. For technical parts, the better baseline may be an approved drawing plus inspection report rather than one physical unit. The principle is the same: production needs an objective reference.

06

Release production only after open issues are closed

Create a short approval record listing what is accepted, what remains conditional and what must change before bulk production. If a defect is accepted only for the sample, state that explicitly. This prevents the factory from interpreting buyer silence as approval of every observed characteristic.
