China sourcing buyer guide

How to source products from China without turning the order into a long chain of assumptions.

Sourcing from China works best when the buyer treats it as a sequence of controlled decisions rather than one supplier search. A factory name is only the beginning. The durable process is to define the product, compare suppliers on the same basis, verify who will make it, approve a production benchmark, control changes and inspect before the goods are released.

Buyer control points

A process that can be checked, not guessed.

Use this guide as an operating framework and adapt the depth of control to product risk, order value, customization and destination-market requirements.

Working checklist

  • Product specification or reference
  • Quantity by SKU
  • Target market and compliance requirements
  • Comparable supplier RFQ
  • Supplier verification evidence
  • Approved sample or drawing revision
  • Production milestone record
  • Inspection checklist and result
  • Final packing list and export handoff
01

1. Start with a quote-ready product brief

Before contacting suppliers, write down what must be true about the product. Include reference photos or drawings, quantity by SKU, material, dimensions, finish, function, packaging, destination market and required timing. For a custom product, distinguish fixed requirements from areas where the supplier may propose alternatives. A weak brief creates quotations that look comparable but are actually based on different products.

02

2. Search by manufacturing capability, not just product name

A marketplace search often ranks visibility rather than technical fit. For standard consumer products, catalogue similarity may be enough for a first screen. For industrial, electrical or custom products, the search should follow the manufacturing process: machining, injection molding, sheet metal, cable assembly, glass forming, electronics integration or another relevant capability. Keep a broader candidate pool first, then remove mismatches.

03

3. Verify the supplier behind the quotation

Confirm the legal entity, the role of the company, where critical production occurs, and whether the supplier can show evidence that matches your product. A trading company is not automatically bad and a factory is not automatically good; the important question is whether responsibilities and production ownership are visible. High-risk orders deserve deeper verification before deposits, tooling or large commitments.

04

4. Normalize the RFQ before comparing price

Price comparison is useful only when suppliers quote the same specification, quantity, packaging, Incoterm, tooling assumption, testing scope and documentation. Ask questions in a structured matrix and force ambiguous items into explicit yes/no or quantified answers. A higher unit price with included tooling, better packaging or stronger inspection evidence may be cheaper than a low headline price with exclusions.

05

5. Turn the approved sample into a production baseline

A good sample proves possibility, not repeatability. Record exactly what was approved: material, dimensions, color or finish, component version, artwork, accessories, packaging and functional behavior. If the product changes after the sample, create a new revision rather than relying on chat history. This baseline is what production and inspection should reference.

06

6. Control production through milestones

Do not wait until the promised ship date to ask whether production is finished. Track key milestones such as material preparation, tooling, first production, packaging completion and inspection readiness. For custom products, require written approval for substitutions or changes that affect material, component, dimensions, firmware, finish or packaging.

07

7. Inspect against objective acceptance criteria

A useful inspection answers whether named requirements conform. Define critical dimensions, function, visual standards, quantity, labeling, packaging and required documents before the inspector arrives. If a defect appears, decide whether it requires rework, sorting, concession or rejection and preserve that decision in the order record.

08

8. Plan logistics before the goods are ready

Confirm carton dimensions, weights, dangerous-goods or special handling questions, commercial invoice data, packing list format and pickup terms early. Multi-supplier orders need an agreed consolidation plan so factories do not independently create conflicting shipping marks, paperwork or delivery dates. The sourcing process is complete only when the goods and documentation are ready for the chosen freight handoff.
